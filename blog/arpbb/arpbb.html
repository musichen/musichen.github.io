Abstract

"Computers are now being used to generate news stories about company earnings results or economic statistics as they are released. And this almost instantaneous information forms a direct feed into other computers which trade on the news." — Financial Times

This thesis explores how modern software solutions facilitate automated decision-making on stock markets. It proposes and implements an Adaptive Reversal Parametric Breakout strategy based on a Daily Volatility Filter, deployed via a C#/S# + Wealth-Lab automated trading system. The strategy was tested on 80 NYSE and MICEX stocks, filtered to a 25-stock NYSE portfolio, and backtested on 2.5 years of real market data — generating 75.9% net profit with a 13.5% maximum drawdown.

Research Framework

Five Research Questions

RQ 01
Active or passive portfolio management?
Markets are inefficient (Haugen 1995; Rosenberg & Reid 1998). Active management exploits inefficiencies — automated systems are essential to detect them in real-time.
RQ 02
Technical or fundamental analysis?
FA suits passive "buy & hold." TA suits active trading — it is formalizable, computationally tractable, and automatable. TA is the foundation for trading robots.
RQ 03
What trading strategies exist & how to classify them?
Two broad classes: Execution (Iceberg, TWAP, VWAP — order splitting) and Speculative (Trendline, Pullback, Breakout, Arbitrage, Scalping). Breakout offers the best automation trade-off.
RQ 04
What platform & architecture for trading robots?
C# + S# (Stock Sharp) + Wealth-Lab provides multi-core HFT support, DMA connectivity, and emulation >1M events/sec. Six infrastructure options analyzed (A–F), with DMA recommended.
RQ 05
Which strategy to choose for successful automated trading?
The ARP-Breakout strategy: simple, reversible, adaptive. It learns the daily trading range via a volatility filter, enters on breakouts, and manages positions with trailing stops tied to ATR — all controlled through 14 optimized parameters.

Portfolio Selection

Stock Filtering: NYSE vs. MICEX

80 stocks tested across two exchanges. Only those with non-negative strategy returns were selected into the final portfolio.

Filtering Principle: Rather than selecting by P/E ratio or market cap, stocks are selected by strategy compatibility — the strategy itself determines which stocks belong in the portfolio. The portfolio can be rebalanced periodically by re-running the filter.

Trading Robots

Advantages & Disadvantages

Advantages
  • Speed. Monitors hundreds of securities simultaneously, makes decisions and executes orders in milliseconds.
  • Accuracy. No button-press mistakes, no misread prices, no calculation errors.
  • Inexhaustibility. Works 24/7 without fatigue — never misses signals from exhaustion or loss of concentration.
  • No Emotions. Follows the algorithm precisely — never hesitates to cut losses, never gets greedy on winning streaks.
  • Scalability. Can simultaneously trade multiple strategies across hundreds of instruments.
Disadvantages
  • Cost. Professional robots cost €1,000–5,000+ to develop. HFT may require colocation in exchange data centers.
  • System Failures. If code has a bug, the robot repeats the same mistake systematically — multiplying losses.
  • Rigidity. Cannot adapt to unprecedented market events outside its training parameters.
  • No Judgment. Will drain capital following strategy signals during black swan events — requires human oversight.
  • Over-optimization Risk. A curve-fitted strategy may excel in backtests but fail on future data.
Thesis Roadmap

Seven Chapters

1
Introduction

Problem definition, 5 research questions, goals, motivation, and scope. Target: individual traders, hedge fund managers, banks, and financial institutions.

2
Automated Trading on Stock Market

Defines algorithmic trading. Quantifies market impact: 30–70% of Wall Street volume, 60% on Russian exchanges. Discusses quotation vs. market orders and exchange protective mechanisms against HFT overload.

3
Trading Robots: Types & Platforms

Covers automatic vs. semi-automatic robots (advisers, scanners, utilities). Evaluates platforms: QPILE, Excel+VBA, C++/Delphi, C#/S#+WealthLab. Presents 6 architecture options (A–F) from internet-connected broker to DMA colocation.

4
Stock Market Analysis Tools

Fundamental vs. technical analysis. Deep coverage of indicators: Moving Averages, Oscillators, RSI, Bollinger Bands, MACD, Fibonacci levels. Reviews MetaStock, Wealth-Lab, Omega as software facilitators.

5
Trading Strategies Overview

Classification: execution strategies (Iceberg, TWAP, VWAP) vs. speculative strategies (Trendline, Pullback, Breakout, Arbitrage, Scalping). Evaluates each for automation difficulty and speed requirements.

6
ARP-Breakout Strategy Implementation

The practical core: tool selection (QUIK + Wealth-Lab + C#/S#), architecture design, 14-parameter strategy description, Monte Carlo optimization, stock filtering (80 → 25), portfolio backtest over 2.5 years of NYSE data.

7
Conclusions & Further Research

Summarizes advantages/disadvantages of trading robots. Proposes future directions: multi-algorithm trading, strategy portfolios, strategy-of-strategies evaluation, automated market inefficiency detection.

Future Directions

Suggestions for Further Research

Multi-Algorithm Paradigm

This thesis used a mono-algorithm approach. Compare performance of single-strategy vs. multi-strategy automated systems. How do parallel strategies interact on the same portfolio?

Strategy Portfolios

Instead of a portfolio of stocks, create a portfolio of strategies — trade the same instrument with multiple complementary strategies simultaneously.

Strategy Evaluation

Develop new dimensions for evaluating strategy performance beyond Sharpe ratio and recovery factor. How to measure strategy vitality over changing market regimes?

Automated Inefficiency Detection

If markets are increasingly inefficient (Haugen 1995; Rosenberg & Reid 1998), can we automate the discovery of new market inefficiencies from real-time data? Strategy-to-generate-strategies.
