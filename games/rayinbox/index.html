Theoretical background of this simulation


    My simulation aims to visualize the trajectory of a ray (or light beam) traveling within a rectangular box, reflecting off the walls according to the classical law of reflection (angle of incidence equals angle of reflection). The key objective is to observe whether, over an infinite period, the ray's path becomes periodic—returning to its starting point and repeating its pattern—or if it exhibits more complex, non-repeating behavior.

    Mathematical Background: Theoretical Foundations
  
  The study of ray trajectories in bounded domains, such as a rectangular box, falls under the branch of mathematics known as  dynamical systems , and more specifically,  mathematical billiards . Here are some foundational concepts, theorems, and works related to my simulation:
  
  1.  Mathematical Billiards: 
     -  Definition:  Mathematical billiards involve studying the motion of a point particle (the "billiard ball") within a domain where it moves in straight lines and reflects off the boundary according to the law of reflection.
     -  Relevance:  My simulation models a billiard system where the rectangle is the billiard table, and the ray represents the billiard ball's trajectory.
  
  2.  Periodic and Non-Periodic Orbits: 
     -  Periodic Orbit:  A path that eventually repeats itself after a finite number of reflections.
     -  Non-Periodic (Dense) Orbit:  A path that never exactly repeats but densely fills a region of the space.
     -  Rational vs. Irrational Slopes: 
       -  Rational Slope:  If the angle of the ray leads to a slope that is a rational multiple relative to the sides of the rectangle, the orbit is typically periodic.
       -  Irrational Slope:  Leads to non-periodic or dense orbits where the path never exactly repeats.
  
  3.  Theorem: Periodicity in Rational Billiards 
     -  Statement:  In a rectangular billiard table, if the ratio of the sides is rational and the initial angle leads to a rational slope, the billiard path is periodic.
     -  Implication:  Under certain conditions (rational ratios and slopes), the ray will repeat its path and return to the starting point after a finite number of reflections.
  
  4.  Ergodic Theory: 
     -  Definition:  Studies the statistical behavior of dynamical systems under repeated iterations.
     -  Relevance:  For billiard systems with irrational parameters, ergodic theory explores how the ray's path distributes over the space, often showing dense and non-repeating trajectories.
  
  5.  Notable Works and Mathematicians: 
     -  Leonhard Euler:  Early studies on billiard problems.
     -  Marcel Berger:  Authored "A Panoramic View of Billiards," providing comprehensive coverage of mathematical billiards.
     -  Yuri A. Kifer and Yakov G. Sinai:  Contributed significantly to the understanding of dynamical systems and ergodic theory.
     -  Leonid A. Bunimovich:  Known for work on chaotic billiards and ergodic theory.

    Answer to My Central Question
  


   Will the ray, after an infinite amount of travel time, repeat its pattern and reach the starting point?
  
  The answer depends on specific conditions related to the geometry of the box and the initial angle of the ray:
  
  1.  Rational Ratios and Slopes: 
     -  Box Dimensions:  If the ratio of the box's width to height is a rational number (e.g., 1000px x 2000px, which simplifies to a 1:2 ratio).
     -  Initial Angle:  If the angle of the ray leads to a slope that is rational relative to the box's sides.
     -  Outcome:  The ray will follow a  periodic orbit , meaning it will eventually return to its starting point and repeat its trajectory indefinitely.
  
  2.  Irrational Slopes: 
     -  Initial Angle:  If the slope resulting from the initial angle is irrational relative to the box's sides.
     -  Outcome:  The ray will  never exactly repeat  its path. Instead, it will produce a  dense orbit , filling the available space without ever returning precisely to the starting point.
  
  3.  Mixed Conditions: 
     -  Box Ratios and Slopes:  If the box dimensions and/or the slope combine rational and irrational properties, the behavior can be more complex, potentially leading to quasi-periodic or non-repeating patterns.
  
  4.  Infinite Time Consideration: 
     -  Practical Implications:  While theoretically, with infinite time, periodic orbits will repeat indefinitely, dense orbits in irrational scenarios will never form exact loops, though they come arbitrarily close to any point in the space, including the starting point.
